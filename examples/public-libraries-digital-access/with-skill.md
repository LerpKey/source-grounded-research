# Public Libraries and Digital Access

**Bottom line:** Public libraries are designed to support equitable access to information, learning, culture, and technology, but the evidence in this short review supports a more precise conclusion than “libraries solve the digital divide.” Their contribution is best understood as a local access-and-support layer: they can provide connectivity, devices, digital skills, and human assistance, while broader digital inclusion still depends on affordability, infrastructure, accessibility, and service design beyond the library itself. ([The IFLA-UNESCO Public Library Manifesto 2022](https://repository.ifla.org/bitstreams/d414c76e-17ef-4581-9c0f-cc6e250a2743/download); [What is Digital inclusion? Meaning, Definition.](https://www.unesco.org/en/query-list/d/digital-inclusion))

**As of:** 2026-08-18

## Scope and method

- **Question:** What role can public libraries play in digital access and inclusion?
- **Scope:** Public libraries as community institutions, with emphasis on access, skills, and support rather than a country-by-country impact evaluation.
- **Definitions:** “Digital access” includes connectivity, devices, accessibility, skills, and the ability to use relevant online services. This is an analytical definition for this report, not a single universal statistical standard.
- **Method:** Start with international institutional sources, inspect the original pages, extract only claims supported by those sources, and label recommendations as recommendations.

## Findings

### Libraries have an explicit public-access and learning mandate

The IFLA–UNESCO Public Library Manifesto describes the public library as a local gateway to knowledge and assigns it roles in education, culture, information access, and digital connectivity. The wording establishes a normative and institutional mandate; it does not by itself prove that every library delivers the same level of service or produces a measurable causal effect. ([The IFLA-UNESCO Public Library Manifesto 2022](https://repository.ifla.org/bitstreams/d414c76e-17ef-4581-9c0f-cc6e250a2743/download))

### Digital inclusion is broader than a connection

UNESCO defines digital inclusion as equitable access to technology and the skills needed to use it meaningfully, with particular attention to marginalized groups. This supports treating library connectivity as one component of inclusion rather than a complete solution. ([What is Digital inclusion? Meaning, Definition.](https://www.unesco.org/en/query-list/d/digital-inclusion))

### Libraries can provide a practical local support layer

The manifesto explicitly includes access to information and communication technologies among public-library responsibilities. A reasonable inference is that libraries can function as community access points where people need a place, device, connection, or human assistance. The source does not establish that this support is sufficient for all users or that it has the same impact in every setting. ([The IFLA-UNESCO Public Library Manifesto 2022](https://repository.ifla.org/bitstreams/d414c76e-17ef-4581-9c0f-cc6e250a2743/download))

### Measurement should separate service availability from impact

A useful evaluation should measure at least four distinct layers:

| Layer | Example question | Evidence needed |
|---|---|---|
| Availability | Is a public access point, device, or connection offered? | Service inventory and operating data |
| Use | Who uses the service, how often, and for what tasks? | De-identified usage data or representative surveys |
| Capability | Do users gain skills or complete tasks more successfully? | Pre/post assessment or well-designed user research |
| Outcomes | Does access improve education, employment, participation, or well-being? | Longitudinal or comparison-based evaluation |

This layered model is a synthesis for planning measurement; it is not presented as a finding directly stated by either source.

## Synthesis

**Verified facts:** The international library manifesto gives public libraries an explicit role in equitable information, learning, cultural, and technology access. UNESCO defines digital inclusion in terms of equitable access and meaningful use, not only the existence of a connection. ([The IFLA-UNESCO Public Library Manifesto 2022](https://repository.ifla.org/bitstreams/d414c76e-17ef-4581-9c0f-cc6e250a2743/download); [What is Digital inclusion? Meaning, Definition.](https://www.unesco.org/en/query-list/d/digital-inclusion))

**Inference:** These roles make libraries plausible delivery points for parts of a digital-inclusion strategy, especially where users need local assistance and a trusted physical setting.

**Recommendation:** Funders should avoid evaluating libraries only by the number of public computers or Wi-Fi sessions. A stronger evaluation combines availability, use, capability, and longer-term outcomes.

## Limitations and uncertainties

- This brief does not estimate the causal effect of library services on employment, education, or income.
- The sources are international and institutional; they do not establish how services vary by country, library system, language, disability access, or funding model.
- A full decision report would need local service data, user research, cost information, and outcome evaluations.
