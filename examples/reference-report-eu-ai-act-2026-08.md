# EU AI Act Transparency Rules: What Applies After 2 August 2026?

**Bottom line:** As of 19 August 2026, the EU AI Act’s transparency rules are in their application and enforcement phase for the provisions that took effect on 2 August 2026. The practical duties are not one single “AI label” rule: they differ for systems that interact directly with people, systems that generate synthetic content, deployers that publish deepfakes or certain public-interest text, and providers of general-purpose AI models. Existing systems placed on the market before 2 August 2026 may have a transition until 2 December 2026 for the Article 50(2) marking and detection obligations. ([AI Act implementation timeline](https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/eu-ai-act-implementation-timeline); [AI Act FAQ](https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/faq))

**As of:** 2026-08-19

**Audience:** Product, compliance, communications, and procurement teams operating in or serving the EU.

> This is a source-grounded research brief, not legal advice. The relevant system classification, role, market placement, and national enforcement arrangements must be assessed for the specific deployment.

## Scope and method

This report answers three questions: what transparency rules are currently applicable, who may need to act, and which deadlines remain. It uses the English text of the Regulation, the European Commission’s implementation timeline and guidance, and the AI Act Service Desk FAQ. Commission guidance helps interpret implementation but does not replace the Regulation.

## What the transparency rules cover

### 1. Direct interaction with people

Providers must design and develop systems intended to interact directly with natural persons so that people are informed that they are interacting with an AI system, unless that is obvious from the circumstances and context. The obligation is stated in Article 50(1) of the Regulation and is reflected in the Commission’s explanation of Article 50. ([Regulation (EU) 2024/1689](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj?locale=en); [Navigating the AI Act](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/faqs/navigating-ai-act))

### 2. Synthetic audio, image, video, and text

Providers of AI systems that generate synthetic audio, image, video, or text must ensure that outputs are marked in a machine-readable format and detectable as artificially generated or manipulated, subject to the Regulation’s technical-feasibility and exception language. The Commission says these transparency obligations apply from 2 August 2026. ([Regulation (EU) 2024/1689](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj?locale=en); [Commission transparency guidelines](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/library/guidelines-transparency-obligations-providers-and-deployers-ai-systems))

The transition point matters: the Service Desk FAQ says providers of systems already placed on the market before 2 August 2026 have until 2 December 2026 for the marking and detection obligation under Article 50(2). This is a specific transition, not a general postponement of every AI Act duty. ([AI Act FAQ](https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/faq))

### 3. Deepfakes and certain public-interest text

Deployers of systems that generate or manipulate image, audio, or video content that qualifies as a deepfake must visibly disclose the artificial origin. The Commission also describes a disclosure requirement for AI-generated or manipulated text published to inform the public about matters of public interest, subject to the conditions and exceptions in the Act. ([Navigating the AI Act](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/faqs/navigating-ai-act); [Regulation (EU) 2024/1689](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj?locale=en))

This category is not equivalent to labeling every private or internal use of a text-generation tool. The purpose of publication, the nature of the content, human review, editorial control, and the role of the organization all matter.

### 4. General-purpose AI providers

The Act also contains obligations for providers of general-purpose AI models, including documentation, copyright policy, training-content summaries, and additional duties for models with systemic risk. The Commission’s guidance states that its enforcement powers for these obligations enter into application on 2 August 2026. ([Guidelines for GPAI providers](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/faqs/guidelines-obligations-general-purpose-ai-providers))

## Practical implications by role

| Role | Immediate review question | Evidence to retain |
|---|---|---|
| Model or system provider | Does the system fall within Article 50, and how are outputs marked or detected? | Technical design, labeling tests, exception analysis, change records |
| Professional deployer | Does the deployment expose people to an AI interaction, biometric/emotion system, deepfake, or public-interest publication? | User notices, content workflow, human review, disclosure controls |
| Publisher or communications team | Is AI-generated or manipulated content published to inform the public on a matter of public interest? | Editorial responsibility, review record, disclosure decision |
| Procurement or compliance team | Are provider representations specific enough for the intended EU use? | Contract terms, provider documentation, assessment and monitoring plan |

This table is an operational synthesis from the cited legal and Commission materials. It is not a substitute for a formal legal classification.

## What the August milestone does not mean

- It does not mean every AI system must display the same public label.
- It does not mean all high-risk AI obligations began on 2 August 2026; the timeline contains later high-risk milestones. ([AI Act implementation timeline](https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/eu-ai-act-implementation-timeline))
- It does not make the Commission’s Code of Practice the only route to compliance. The Commission describes the Code as a voluntary compliance tool; non-signatories remain responsible for complying through other adequate means. ([Signing the Code of Practice](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/faqs/signing-code-practice-transparency-ai-generated-content))
- It does not remove the need to analyze exceptions, technical feasibility, human review, editorial control, or the exact role of the organization.

## Recommended next steps

1. Inventory AI systems and model providers used in EU-facing products or workflows. ([Commission transparency guidelines](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/library/guidelines-transparency-obligations-providers-and-deployers-ai-systems))
2. Classify each use by interaction, output type, deployment role, publication purpose, and relevant exception. ([Navigating the AI Act](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/faqs/navigating-ai-act); [Regulation (EU) 2024/1689](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj?locale=en))
3. Test machine-readable marking, detection, visible disclosure, and user-notice controls. ([Commission transparency guidelines](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/library/guidelines-transparency-obligations-providers-and-deployers-ai-systems))
4. Record the reasoning and evidence for each classification. ([Commission transparency guidelines](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/library/guidelines-transparency-obligations-providers-and-deployers-ai-systems))
5. Review pre-2 August 2026 systems against the 2 December 2026 transition deadline. ([AI Act FAQ](https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/faq))
6. Treat the Code of Practice as implementation guidance, not as a substitute for a legal compliance assessment. ([Signing the Code of Practice](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/faqs/signing-code-practice-transparency-ai-generated-content))

## Limitations and uncertainties

- The report does not classify a particular product or deployment.
- Commission guidance and FAQs may be updated as standards, codes, and enforcement practice develop.
- National authorities may publish additional implementation material.
- The legal result can change with facts that are not visible from a public product description, including provider role, market placement, content purpose, and human editorial control.
